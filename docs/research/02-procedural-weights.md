# Survey 2 — Procedural weights, seeds and sharing: does a FloppyLM already exist?

Consolidated from web research on 2026-09-30.

> **Framed for thesis v0.1.** Its "Minimum experiment" section predates [concept v0.2](../concept.md); E0–E4 are defined only by the [roadmap](../roadmap.md).

## Question

Is there already an LM trained from scratch whose total description (weights + tokenizer + runtime) fits
in ≤1.44 MB (1 474 560 bytes) by exploiting the bits-at-rest / RAM-at-runtime asymmetry, i.e. by storing
a short description of a much larger effective model?

## What exists

**"Linear combination of pseudo-random bases" family.** PRANC
([arXiv:2206.08464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08464), ICCV 2023) reparameterizes a whole network as a
linear combination of frozen random networks generated from a single seed; only the coefficients are
trained, from scratch, and the weights are regenerated layer by layer at inference. This is exactly the
FloppyLM mechanism, but it is validated only on image classification (small ResNets, ~100× compression)
and implicit representations, never on an LM. NOLA ([arXiv:2310.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02556),
ICLR 2024) brings the same idea to LoRA matrices: coefficients over frozen random bases, up to ~20× more
compact than rank-1 LoRA on LLaMA-2 70B; it is fine-tuning of a pre-existing dense model, so the total
description includes the backbone. MCNC ([arXiv:2406.19301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19301), ICLR 2025) replaces the linear subspace with a nonlinear manifold generated by a frozen sinusoidal
MLP: it trains ResNet/DeiT-Ti from scratch on CIFAR and ImageNet-100 and does PEFT on LLaMA-2 (25k
parameters, 45.8 MMLU), and the authors explicitly write that they have not shown the effect of MCNC "in
training LLMs from scratch, which could be the most important impact". Kilobyte Models
([arXiv:2608.00860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00860), August 2026) formalizes "network = seed +
quantized latent + normalizations" with a seeded block basis and QAT, obtaining 98.6% on MNIST in 2 KB
and a ResNet-50 adapter in 4 KB; here too the authors state that the method "is not yet extended to
Transformers and LLMs".

**"Seed per block, post-training" family.** SeedLM (Apple,
[arXiv:2410.10714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10714), ICLR 2025) compresses already-trained LLMs: for
each block of 8–12 weights it brute-forces one of 2^16−1 seeds of an LFSR, whose random matrix, combined
with 3–4 coefficients at 4 bits and a shared 4-bit exponent, reconstructs the block, at 3–4 bits/weight
(Llama 3 70B: WikiText-2 ppl 2.9 FP16 → 3.8 at 4 bits, 5.7 at 3 bits). It shows that an LFSR is a cheap
generator in hardware and that the compute-for-memory trade works, but it does not go below ~3
bits/weight, is not training from scratch and does not make the effective model larger than the stored
one. Hyper-Compression ([arXiv:2409.00592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00592)) represents parameters
with trajectories of ergodic dynamical systems, post hoc, at performance close to int4 on LLaMA/Qwen.
Neural Weight Compression ([arXiv:2510.11234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11234)) learns a neural codec
for LM weights, strong in the 4–6-bit regime. All operate on already-trained models.

**Hashing and hypernetworks.** HashedNets ([arXiv:1504.04788](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04788)) shares
weights via hashing: no stored index, up to 1/64 on MNIST; it is the direct precursor of "large effective
model, few free parameters". HyperNetworks
([arXiv:1609.09106](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09106)) generates weights with an auxiliary network; on
enwik8 the HyperLSTM reaches ~1.34 bpc, but for LMs the hypernetwork is used for dynamic weights, not to
reduce bytes at rest. Irie & Schmidhuber ([arXiv:2112.15545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15545)) train
character-level LSTMs on enwik8 with matrices parameterized by DCT coefficients: it is the closest
precedent of "LM trained in a compressed weight space", at small scale and without total byte
accounting.

**Weight sharing and recurrent depth.** ALBERT
([arXiv:1909.11942](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11942)) and Universal Transformers
([arXiv:1807.03819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819)) are the simplest form of "short description,
deep effective model". Relaxed Recursive Transformers
([arXiv:2410.20672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20672), ICLR 2025) convert Gemma 2B into a 1B recursive
model with per-depth LoRA that beats TinyLlama 1.1B and Pythia 1B, but starting from pretrained weights.
Mixture-of-Recursions ([arXiv:2507.10524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524)) trains recursive models
from 135M to 1.7B from scratch and, at equal FLOPs and with fewer unique parameters, improves perplexity
and few-shot over vanilla and simple recursive models. It is the family with the best positive
from-scratch evidence for LMs, but no work measures it in total bytes under 2 MB.

**Intrinsic dimension and frozen random networks.** Li et al.
([arXiv:1804.08838](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08838)) train from scratch in random subspaces: d_int90
≈ 750 for an MNIST MLP and 290 for a LeNet, i.e. seed + 750 floats, ~150–260× compression; they note
that 90% of performance is a weak threshold. Aghajanyan et al.
([arXiv:2012.13255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)) show that 200 projected parameters are enough to
bring RoBERTa to 90% on MRPC, but in fine-tuning: the intrinsic dimension is low precisely because
pretraining has already done the work, so it does not transfer to training from scratch. On the "frozen
random" side, Shen et al. ([arXiv:2109.03939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03939)) find supermasks in a
random one-layer Transformer that reach 29.45 BLEU on IWSLT14; Zhong & Andreas
([arXiv:2410.04368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04368), NeurIPS 2024) show that random Transformers with
only embedding/unembedding trained learn modular arithmetic, associative recall and "some aspects" of
text generation. On the tokenizer, [arXiv:2605.09751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09751) replaces the
input embedding table with fixed binary codes without measurable loss (ppl 2.36 vs 2.44, within
seed-to-seed variance), which makes the input table free in bytes.

**The closest counter-example: OpenAI Parameter Golf (March–April 2026).** The challenge
([repo](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf), analysis
[arXiv:2607.01517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01517)) asks for the best LM with an artifact ≤16 MB
counting code + compressed weights, evaluated in bpb on FineWeb, with 10 minutes on 8×H100 (plus a
non-record track with unlimited compute). OpenAI explicitly listed "Learning adapters on random linear
maps" among the requested PRs. This produced exactly FloppyLM's experiments, in a byte regime 11× wider.
PR [#1113](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf/pull/1113): random orthogonal matrices regenerated
from a seed (0 bytes) + rank-32 LoRA, 29.7M effective parameters of which 3.74M stored, artifact
5 191 021 bytes, 1.3705 bpb. PR [#1753](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf/pull/1753): network
frozen from a uint32 seed + learned binary supermasks (~118M mask bits) + fp16 scales, 14.9 MB, 1.2917
bpb after 57 h on A100; it notes that the mask bits are almost incompressible. PR
[#2058](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf/pull/2058) (rank-160 adapters on random MLPs, within
16 MB) reaches 1.1971; PR [#1710](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf/pull/1710) uses random-feature
MLPs (input projection from a seed) inside a competitive 13.4 MB stack. PR
[#336](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf/pull/336) prototypes a shared-trunk hypernetwork that
generates 26.5M GPT parameters from 2.8M (2.09 MB) without reporting bpb. PR
[#1589](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf/pull/1589) documents systematic failures on 1×H100 in
10 minutes: a "seed model" θ = θ₀ + P·φ with φ ∈ R^2048 (547k stored parameters, 8064× expansion) does
not even complete the first validation because generating P block by block dominates the step;
coordinate→weight SIREN generator 5.12 bpb; neural cellular automaton 4.16; rank-32 hypernetwork 2.74.
The most instructive near-paired comparison is by the same author with the same pipeline: Universal
Transformer 3 blocks × 4 iterations, PR [#1110](https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf/pull/1110),
4 946 680 bytes, 1.2249 bpb, versus random maps + LoRA at 5.19 MB, 1.3705 bpb. At almost equal bytes,
weight sharing beats seeded weights by 0.15 bpb.

**Floppy as an explicit constraint.** On 13 September 2026 an HF contributor published "Single Floppy
346K" ([write-up](https://runtimewire.com/article/nilky-single-floppy-346k-language-model-raspberry-pi),
[model](https://huggingface.co/NILKNARFGonzo/single-floppy-346k)): a 346k-parameter dense LM designed to
fit on a floppy, declared worse than a 1k model, with no metric and no byte accounting. The llama2.c
stories260K (≈1.06 MB FP32) also already fits on a floppy. "An LM on a floppy" in itself is therefore not
new; the possible novelty lies only in the quality/byte ratio.

## What is missing

I actively searched for counter-examples with these queries and sources: "language model fits on a
floppy disk", "procedurally generated weights language model from scratch", "seed-based weight
generation LLM 2026", "pretraining in random subspace / random basis coefficients", "hypernetwork
generates transformer weights compression", "demoscene 64k neural network", "description length under
1 MB TinyStories", plus targeted search in the Parameter Golf PRs (hypernetwork, seed, random weights,
procedural, PRNG, floppy, 1MB/2MB/4MB, ternary, entropy). The result is clear on four points.

First, the academic literature on pseudo-random bases (PRANC, NOLA, MCNC, Kilobyte Models) has never
trained an LM from scratch, and two of these works explicitly state it as a limitation. Second, the
seed-based LLM works (SeedLM, Hyper-Compression, NWC) are post-training and stay at ≥3 bits/weight on the
stored model, with no expansion to a larger effective model. Third, Parameter Golf has already tested the
central idea on real LMs. It did not, however, test it in FloppyLM's regime: 16 MB budget instead of
1.44, a 10-minute constraint that penalizes precisely weight expansion (the #1589 failures are
compute failures, not bit failures), procedural coefficients not entropy-coded (#1113 spends ~10.9 bits
per stored parameter, 5.1 MB for 3.74M parameters), no rate-aware loss on the coefficients, no
measurement of boot cost, FineWeb evaluation instead of TinyStories-style coherence. Fourth, no work
found compares, at exactly equal total bytes, with entropy coding for all, a procedural model against a
ternary/2-bit dense model plus a weight-sharing variant under 1.5 MB.

The residual gap is therefore narrow and precise: an LM trained from scratch, total description
≤1 474 560 bytes including tokenizer and runtime, with effective parameters ≫ stored bits, trained with
an MDL loss on the description bits, compared on equal terms with low-bit dense and with weight sharing.
The idea "seeded weights + adapters for an LM under a byte constraint" is not new; what remains open is
only whether it works under 1.5 MB and better than the alternatives.

## Implication for the thesis

Conditions touched. **F0 (novelty)**: see verdict below. **F1**: the only existing near-paired
comparison (#1110 vs #1113, ~5 MB) goes against the thesis: depth recursion beats seeded weights + LoRA
by 0.15 bpb. It is a single seed, at 10 minutes and with coefficients not optimized in bits, so it does
not kill F1, but it shifts the burden of proof. **F3**: #1589 shows that dense expansion θ₀ + P·φ with
explicit P is prohibitive; structured generators are needed (per-block LFSR as in SeedLM,
Fastfood/Hadamard, Kronecker), generated once at boot and not at every step, as Li 2018 already noted.
**F2**: PRANC/MCNC compare against pruning and quantization, never against a dense model with the same
entropy coding. It follows that the thesis must be reformulated: "procedural" must include weight
sharing (recursion plus per-depth LoRA, Relaxed Recursive/MoR family) as the main arm, not as a
baseline. Seeded bases should be seen as an orthogonal addition (free random features in MLPs, fixed
binary codes for the input), not as a full replacement of the weights.

**F0 verdict: partial.** It does not fully trigger because the bounded literature search has not identified a work that trains from scratch an LM with
total description ≤1.44 MB and effective model ≫ stored bits, with rate-aware loss and measured boot;
the academic literature explicitly leaves it open (MCNC, Kilobyte Models). One cannot, however, say "it
does not trigger": Parameter Golf has publicly formulated and implemented the same underlying idea (seed
→ frozen weights → learned adapters, 30M effective in 5.19 MB; supermasks on a seeded network;
hypernetwork 26.5M from 2.09 MB). The novelty claim must therefore narrow to the regime (under 1.5 MB,
complete data-floppy artifact, real FAT12), the method (MDL training of the coefficients with entropy
coding, equal-byte comparison against ternary dense and recursive) and the measurement (boot cost and
TinyStories coherence), and must cite Parameter Golf as the closest state of the art.

## Minimum experiment

The gap is attacked with E0+E1 at reduced scale before anything else. E0 builds the dense frontier on
TinyStories with byte-exact accounting: int4/ternary dense and Universal-Transformer-style recursive (3
blocks × k iterations, iteration embedding), all with runtime and tokenizer counted, at budgets of 0.5,
1.0 and 1.44 MB. E1 trains three procedural variants at exactly equal bytes: (a) block-wise
PRANC/Kilobyte with a per-block LFSR seed and 4-bit coefficients, (b) seeded random matrices + per-depth
LoRA (reduced #1113), (c) recursive + shared random basis. The F1 kill criterion is: if no procedural
variant beats the best of dense and recursive in validation bpb at 1.44 MB with ≥3 seeds, the
procedural-weights thesis falls and only weight sharing remains. Expansion cost must be measured
immediately (boot time on the reference CPU, peak RAM), because it is the point where Parameter Golf saw
all generator variants fail. E2 then adds entropy coding and the rate loss to all arms. Only if E1/E2
give a margin does it make sense to scale the effective parameters (E3) and produce the FAT12 image (E4).
